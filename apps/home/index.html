01 — The Shift

Software → Work

The next platform shift is not about which model is smartest. It is about where work happens — and who is accountable when it does.

01

The center of gravity is moving off raw compute and model scores, onto agents, workflow and the outcome they produce.

02

Software stops being a tool a person operates. A person states intent; the work gets executed and delivered back.

02 — The Problem

Today AI answers well. The work still falls apart.

Every step works on its own. The work as a whole has no shared state, no evidence and no memory.

A question goes into chat. The answer comes back. Someone re-types it into the real application. Someone else reviews it in their head. The result lands in a document nobody opens again. Between every pair of steps, context evaporates — and nothing that happened can be proven later.

The same five steps, except nothing is dropped between them. Context carries forward, every action is taken inside a boundary, every result leaves evidence, and what was learned becomes the starting point of the next run rather than being thrown away.

Context evaporates

What the model knew at 10:04 is gone at 10:06. The next person — or the next agent — starts from nothing.

Action is disconnected

The answer is in one window, the system of record in another. A human is the integration layer, by hand, every time.

Nothing can be proven

“It looks right” is not verification. Without evidence there is no accountability, and without accountability AI never gets the important work.

07 — Architecture

Five layers. The top moves fast. The middle must not move at all.

Pick a layer to see what changing it would disturb. Models are replaceable and tools are composable. Your semantics, your evidence and your organizational memory are the part you cannot afford to lose.

Replace a surface and nothing below notices. The experience layer is where you should be able to move fastest.

Swap the model — frontier, domestic or fully local — and nothing below this line changes. Your ontology, your evidence and your permissions are untouched.

Change the harness and everything above it has to be re-verified. This is where authorization, execution, evidence and rollback live, so it is the layer that must not move casually.

Change how your organization is modeled and every layer above inherits the change. This is the most expensive thing in the system to get wrong, and the most valuable to get right.

Move between cloud, on-premise and local-first without touching anything above. Deployment is a choice, not a fork of the product.

Why the split is where it is

Everything above the harness is meant to be replaced — surfaces, models, tools, even the vendor. Everything at and below it is the part that is yours: how your organization is modeled, what was decided, what was proven, who was allowed. Put the line anywhere else and you either cannot move, or you lose the thing that made moving worthwhile.

Five stacked layers, from infrastructure at the base through ontology and knowledge, the harness, agents and workflow, up to the experience layer. Select a layer to see what changing it would disturb.

08 — Trust

An agent you cannot audit is an agent you cannot deploy.

The harness is what turns a capable model into an action your organization is willing to be accountable for.

Every action passes six gates: authorize, execute, observe, verify, evidence, and — when verification fails — rollback. The run above shows a failing check being caught and reversed. That path is the point: a system that can only succeed cannot be trusted.

Memory is more than transcripts

A chat log tells you what was said. Organizational memory tells you what was decided, on what evidence, what was done about it, what the result was, and what everyone learned. That is the part a competitor cannot copy and a model upgrade cannot replace.

Organizational memory = decision + evidence + action + result + learning

A knowledge graph linking people, projects, decisions, evidence, actions, artifacts and memory.

And the person who never signs in

Most of the people a result has to convince will never open this product. They are handed a document, in a meeting, by someone they trust, and they have to decide. Everything above is worth nothing to them unless it survives the trip into the artifact — so these are requirements on what comes out, not on the runtime. We are designing to them now; a result that cannot meet them is not finished.

You can tell what was generatedWhich passages a model wrote, and which a person did, marked in the document itself — not in a settings page nobody opens.
Every claim opens to its sourceDoubt a sentence, click it, and the original is there. If a conclusion cannot be opened, that is itself the finding.
Uncertainty is shown, not smoothedConfidence and what is still unknown are part of the result. A document with no visible doubt is not more trustworthy; it is less.
09 — Where it starts

Start where the work is valuable and the result can be checked.

Digital inputs, a decomposable process, a verifiable result, a governable risk. Those four together are what makes agentic work real rather than impressive — and they show up in very different disciplines.

Reviewing an inbound contract against your own playbook.

Who is in the room
A clause-extraction agent, a reviewer agent that holds the playbook, a drafting agent for the redline — and the lawyer who decides what is actually acceptable.
What gets checked
Every deviation is matched to the playbook clause it departs from, and every extracted term points back to its location in the source document. A clause the system cannot place is flagged, not guessed.
What is left behind
The position taken, the reason, and where it landed in negotiation. The next contract of the same shape starts from that, not from the playbook alone.

Getting from twenty interviews to a problem worth solving.

Who is in the room
Agents that transcribe, cluster and challenge; a facilitator who owns the framing. Divergence is cheap for agents and convergence is a human judgment — the tool should not blur which is which.
What gets checked
Every insight carries the interview fragments it came from, so a cluster that sounds good and rests on one quote is visible as exactly that. Concepts are checked against the constraints that were agreed up front.
What is left behind
The problem definition, the directions that were rejected, and why. Most design knowledge is lost in the second one; a canvas that keeps it is worth more than the deck.

A student investigating a question they cannot look up.

Who is in the room
A guide agent that asks the next question instead of answering, an evidence agent that checks whether a claim is supported, the student, and a teacher who can see the whole path.
What gets checked
A conclusion without evidence attached does not count as finished — the same rule the rest of this system runs on. The agents are configured to withhold answers, which is a permission setting, not a promise.
What is left behind
The reasoning is the artifact. A teacher reads the path — what was tried, what was discarded, where the student changed their mind — rather than grading a paragraph that could have come from anywhere.

Turning a month of numbers into a decision someone will sign.

Who is in the room
Agents that pull from the systems of record, build the model and re-derive it independently, plus the analyst who owns the assumptions.
What gets checked
The model is recomputed against actuals, and every assumption without a source behind it is marked as needing a human. An unproven number is allowed to exist; it is not allowed to be silent.
What is left behind
The definitions used and the judgments made become next month’s baseline, so the recurring argument about what a number means happens once.

Due diligence, or a market you have two weeks to understand.

Who is in the room
A research agent team working many sources in parallel, an analyst agent, a writer — and a consultant whose method is the thing worth keeping.
What gets checked
Sources are verifiable and the rejected ones are recorded with the reason. Knowing what was looked at and thrown away is most of what makes a finding trustworthy.
What is left behind
The method becomes an agent and a skill the client keeps running after the engagement ends. That is the difference between selling a report and leaving a system.

A routine process that crosses four systems and breaks in one.

Who is in the room
Agents granted exactly the tools the process needs and nothing else, plus the operator who owns the exceptions.
What gets checked
Every call happens inside an authorization boundary, is observed while it runs, and has a rollback path when verification fails. This is the case where the harness stops being a design principle and starts being the reason you are allowed to deploy at all.
What is left behind
Exceptions become rules. The fourth time the same thing breaks, the handling is already part of the workflow.

These are worked examples of how a unit of work is shaped in each discipline — not customer stories. We are early, and we would rather show you the shape of the thing than borrow credibility we have not earned.

10 — The unit of work

“Use AI” is not a deliverable. A unit of work is.

A unit of work has a defined input, a defined output, a boundary someone is responsible for, and a way to be checked. That is what makes it something you can buy, verify, reuse and price — instead of something you can only be impressed by.

What happens when you stop paying

This is the question open source is actually for, and the one most vendors answer only in a contract you read once. Here is the commitment, and beside each line, honestly, how much of it is real today.

  • The open part keeps working. Not a trial that expires. The work layer is yours to keep running, on your own machines, for as long as you want it. Blocked on the licence. There isn’t one yet, so today this is an intention, not a right.
  • Your data comes out whole. Everything you put in and everything the system produced, in open formats, without asking us. Partly built. Individual artifacts export today; one command for the whole workspace does not exist yet.
  • There is a written way down. A stated path from hosted to self-hosted, with a checklist on both sides — not a support ticket and a shrug. Not built. Named in the plan, not yet designed.
  • We do not switch anything off. No dark launch of a kill switch, no feature moved behind a paywall after you built on it. What is open stays open. In force. It is a rule about our own conduct, and the one line here that needs no engineering.

Whatever makes us a neutral work layer is open; whatever accumulates with you is sold. Naming the third pile is the honest part — it is how we run this, not something you are buying, and not something you should have to trust blindly.

13 — Straight answers

The questions that decide whether this is deployable.

Where does our data live?

Wherever you decide. Cloud, your own infrastructure, a hybrid, or local-first on the machine itself — the same code runs in all of them, and deployment is a configuration choice rather than a cut-down build. Edition is a separate question from location: what the commercial edition adds is the governance layer above the work layer — single sign-on, audit, multi-tenancy, compliance export — and you can run that on your own infrastructure too.

What happens when we want to change models?

You change them. Models sit at the runtime layer, above your ontology and below your experience. Swapping a provider — frontier, domestic or fully local — does not touch how your organization’s knowledge is modelled, and does not invalidate the evidence already recorded.

How do we stop an agent doing something it should not?

Every action goes through the harness: an authorization boundary before it runs, observation while it runs, a verification step against a stated standard, and a rollback path when that check fails. An agent cannot reach a tool it was not granted, and it cannot record an outcome it cannot evidence.

Do we have to replace what we already use?

No — that is the opposite of the point. WorkspaceX connects to Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Lark, DingTalk, Slack, GitHub and your systems of record. It is the layer where work runs across them, not a replacement for any of them.

Is this open source?

The repository is public and readable today — that part is simply true, and you can check it. The licence is not: there is no LICENSE file in it yet, so nothing there grants you the right to run or modify it. We are not going to describe that as open source until it is. The intent is open core — the workspace object model, the agent and skill format and the connector interfaces open; governance, reliability and scale commercial — and the licence decision is the next step, not a detail.

What actually leaves the machine when we work locally?

Nothing, and it is enforced below the application rather than by our own good behaviour. Local-only work runs inside a guard that patches the single call that every outbound network connection in the process has to pass through — so a vendor SDK’s telemetry, or an analytics beacon inside a transitive dependency, is refused exactly as our own code would be. A machine on your own network counts as another machine and is refused too. The claim is deliberately narrow, because the narrow one is the true one: while work belongs to a local-only workspace, nothing leaves it. Outside that boundary a normal deployment’s traffic is normal traffic, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

We have to run a security review. What can we ask for?

Today: the source, which is public and readable, and two audits you can run yourself rather than take our word for — a dependency licence inventory and a scan of the history for committed credentials, both scripts in the repository. Not today: there are no releases yet, so there is no signed release with a bill of materials attached, and no offline patch bundle for an air-gapped install. Both are commitments we have written down and neither exists, which is a more useful answer than a questionnaire response that implies they do.

What stage is it at?

Early, and honest about it. We are building with a small number of design partners who have real, verifiable, high-value work — not running a waitlist. If that is you, the next step is a conversation, not a signup form.
